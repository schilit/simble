The speaker

0 SDUs received
Volume State — volume 128 · muted no · change counter 0

This is the control-point idiom, the pattern behind most settable BLE devices. Nothing here sets the volume directly: every control writes an opcode to the write-only Volume Control Point (2B7E), and the device's own script applies it, updates Volume State (2B7D) and bumps the change counter — exactly what a phone does over LE Audio's Volume Control Service. The audio gain follows the characteristic, so what you hear is the GATT value. A connected central can write the same opcodes and the tone changes here.

The audio is streamed through the simulated stack: a second device on the same link is an audio source that sends isochronous SDUs (HCI ISO packets, with real SDU framing and sequence numbers) to the speaker, and the page plays only what the sink actually received — through the volume gain above. Not modeled: CIS establishment (SDUs ride the connection handle) and the codec choice above. With LC3 selected the SDUs carry real LC3 frames — 40 octets per 10 ms, exactly what this device's PAC record advertises and what Android would send — encoded and decoded by the codec compiled into this page. With PCM selected they carry raw samples, which is how SimBLE models the media plane when no codec is present: the payload is opaque to the protocol layers either way.
